Accessibility

Our Commitment to Accessibility

We are committed to ensuring digital accessibility for people with disabilities. We are continually improving the user experience for everyone, and applying the relevant accessibility standards.

Web Users and Web Accessibility

Disability Description Common Barriers Accessibility Principles to Apply
Auditory People who are deaf or hard of hearing. Audio-only content, videos without captions, and missing transcripts. Perceivable: Provide captions, transcripts, and visual alternatives for audio information.
Cognitive and Neurological People with learning disabilities, memory difficulties, attention disorders, or neurological conditions. Complex language, confusing navigation, distracting animations, and inconsistent layouts. Understandable: Use clear language, consistent navigation, simple layouts, and provide options to pause animations.
Physical People who have limited movement, strength, or coordination. Websites that require a mouse, small clickable areas, and time-sensitive interactions. Operable: Make all website features available through keyboard navigation and provide enough time to complete tasks.
Speech People who have difficulty speaking or communicating verbally. Text-only content, lack of voice recognition, and limited communication options. Operable: Provide text-to-speech options, voice recognition, and alternative communication methods.
Visual People who are blind or have low vision. Images without alt text, poor color contrast, and complex layouts. Perceivable: Provide alt text for images, ensure sufficient color contrast, and use simple, consistent layouts.
